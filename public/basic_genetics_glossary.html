Building blocks

Where the hereditary material physically lives

DNA
Deoxyribonucleic acid — the molecule that stores hereditary information as a sequence of four bases (A, T, C, G), organized as a double helix.
Gene
A segment of DNA containing the instructions to build a specific protein or functional RNA. The basic unit of heredity.
Chromosome
A tightly coiled structure of DNA and protein that packages genes. Humans have 46, arranged in 23 pairs.
Chromatin
DNA wound around histone proteins, forming chromosomes. It condenses or relaxes depending on the cell cycle stage.
Locus
The specific physical address of a gene on a chromosome. Plural: loci.
Genome
The complete set of genetic material of an organism — every gene, on every chromosome, together.
Genetic identity

How variation is described in an individual

Allele
One of two or more alternative versions of a gene, arising from sequence variation at the same locus.
Genotype
The specific combination of alleles an individual carries for a gene — e.g. AA, Aa, or aa.
Phenotype
The observable physical, biochemical, or behavioral expression of a genotype, also shaped by environment.
Homozygous
Carrying two identical alleles for a given gene, e.g. AA or aa.
Heterozygous
Carrying two different alleles for a given gene, e.g. Aa.
Wild type
The typical, most common allele or phenotype in a population — used as the reference point for comparison.
Reading a Punnett square Two heterozygous parents (Aa × Aa) produce offspring in a 1:2:1 genotype ratio. A = dominant allele   a = recessive allele. Three of the four boxes carry at least one A, so the dominant phenotype appears in 3 out of 4 offspring — only the aa box shows the recessive phenotype.
Inheritance patterns

Why one allele can outweigh another

Dominant
An allele that produces its phenotype even with only one copy present, masking the recessive allele in a heterozygote.
Recessive
An allele whose phenotype appears only when two copies are present — it is masked by a dominant allele.
Carrier
A heterozygous individual for a recessive allele. They do not show the trait but can pass it to their offspring.
Trait
Any observable characteristic of an organism, resulting from genotype, environment, or both.
Chromosomes & variation

Zooming back out to the whole karyotype

Autosome
Any chromosome that is not a sex chromosome. Humans have 22 pairs of autosomes.
Sex chromosome
The chromosome pair — X and Y in humans — that determines biological sex.
Karyotype
A visual profile of an individual's full set of chromosomes, arranged by size and shape, used to detect structural or numerical abnormalities.
Mutation
A permanent change in a DNA sequence. It can create new alleles, and may or may not affect the phenotype.

Study tip: keep the cascade at the top in mind — most first-year confusion comes from mixing up genotype (the alleles carried) with phenotype (what is actually expressed).
